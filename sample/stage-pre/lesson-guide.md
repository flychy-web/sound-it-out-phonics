# Stage Pre: complete lesson guide (free sample)

Stage Pre is entirely oral. No letters, no print, no writing. You are training your child's ear to hear the sounds inside words, which is the foundation everything else stands on. Four skills, taught in order, kept deliberately short. Sessions run about 10 minutes. Move on when your child gets about 8 of 10 right across two different days: two days matters, one hot streak is not mastery.

The game cards for these skills are in `stage-pre-sample-pack.pdf`.

You will notice the practice words lean on the sounds m, s, a, t, and p. That is deliberate: these are the first five letters your child will meet in Stage 1, so their ears are already trained on those sounds before the letters ever appear.

---

## Skill 1 (pa-01): Rhyme recognition

**Goal:** Your child can hear whether two spoken words rhyme.

**Teach, word for word:** "Rhyming words sound the same at the end. Cat, hat. Listen: caaat, haaat. They rhyme! Cat, dog. Do those sound the same at the end? No, they do not rhyme."

**Practice:** Use the Rhyme Time cards. Read each pair aloud; child gives thumbs up if the pair rhymes, thumbs down if not. Core pairs: cat and hat, sun and fun, dog and log (rhyme); cat and dog, sun and cup (no). When that gets easy, flip it: "Tell me a word that rhymes with sun." Silly made-up words count and should be celebrated.

**Mastery check:** 8 of 10 pairs judged correctly, across two different days.

---

## Skill 2 (pa-02): First sound isolation

**Goal:** Your child can tell you the very first sound in a spoken word.

**Teach:** "Every word starts with a sound. Mat starts with mmm. Sun starts with sss. Listen to my lips: p, p, pig. What sound does pig start with?"

**Practice:** Use the First Sounds cards: mat, sun, pig, top, apple. Say the sound, never the letter name: sss, not ess. If your child answers with a letter name, smile and say "that is the letter's name; I want the sound it makes: sss." Then play it anywhere: "What sound does map start with? Sock? Pan?"

**Mastery check:** 8 of 10 first sounds identified, across two different days.

---

## Skill 3 (pa-03): Oral blending

**Goal:** Your child can glue spoken sounds into a word: s ... a ... t makes sat.

**Teach:** "I am going to say a word in tiny broken sounds and you pull them together. Listen: m ... a. Push them together: ma! Now: a ... t. What word? At!"

**Practice:** Start with the two-sound words (ma, at), then three sounds: sat, mat, tap. Say each sound cleanly, about one second apart, then let your child say the whole word. Keep the gap short at first and stretch it as they improve.

**Mastery check:** 8 of 10 words blended correctly, across two different days.

---

## Skill 4 (pa-04): Oral segmenting

**Goal:** The reverse: your child breaks a whole word into every sound. This is the strongest sign a child is ready for print.

**Teach:** "Now you are the word breaker. I say the whole word, you say every little sound. Sat. Break it: s ... a ... t. Three sounds!"

**Practice:** Use the Sound Box mat. Say a word; your child pushes one counter (a coin, a button, dry cereal) into a box for each sound while saying it. Words: at, mat, sat, tap, pat. Two-box words first, then three.

**Mastery check:** 8 of 10 words segmented with the right counters pushed, across two different days. When this is solid, your child is ready for Stage 1 and their first letters.

---

**A note on sounds:** Say pure sounds without an added vowel: mmm not muh, t not tuh. It feels unnatural for about a day and matters enormously later, because muh-ah-puh blends into muhahpuh, not map.
