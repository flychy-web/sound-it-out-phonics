# Why this sequence works

Reading is not natural the way speech is. Brains learn to read by mapping speech sounds (phonemes) onto letters and letter patterns (graphemes), and fifty years of research says the fastest, most reliable way to build that mapping is explicit, systematic phonics taught in a deliberate order. The National Reading Panel's meta-analysis reached that conclusion in 2000, and the replications since have only strengthened it.

Two ideas drive this curriculum's design.

First, phonemic awareness comes before print. A child who cannot hear that "sun" breaks into three sounds has nothing to attach letters to. Stage Pre is entirely oral for exactly this reason.

Second, orthographic mapping (Linnea Ehri's term for how words become instantly recognizable) only happens when a child sounds words out successfully many times. That means practice text must be decodable: built only from patterns already taught. Guessing from pictures or context, the core move of leveled-reading and three-cueing approaches, actively works against the mapping process, which is why those approaches underperform in study after study.

So the spine rules here are strict. Skills are taught in sequence. Nothing appears in practice before it is taught. Every decodable sentence is programmatically validated against the graphemes covered so far. A child moves on at roughly 80 percent mastery, not on a schedule.

The sequence is anchored to UFLI Foundations, the University of Florida Literacy Institute's openly published scope and sequence, chosen because it is evidence-aligned, field-tested at scale, and free of commercial-program lock-in. This curriculum is independently developed and is not affiliated with or endorsed by UFLI or the University of Florida.
